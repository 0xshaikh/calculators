Frequently asked questions

The questions people actually ask about the calculator.

Will my broker actually give me this leverage?

Not necessarily. LotCal assumes your entire balance is available as initial margin, so the number it shows is the maximum your account could theoretically support. Real brokers cap leverage (e.g. 1:30 for retail forex in some regions, higher for crypto), apply tiers, and set their own margin requirements, spread and maintenance margin. Treat the result as an upper bound and check your broker’s specification before relying on it.

Why isn’t the price live?

LotCal uses reference snapshot prices so the tool works without a live feed and without asking you to connect an account. Replace the price with your broker’s current quote — the calculator only needs a number, and the exact price you’d actually trade at. The P&L per $1 move does not depend on the price at all: it comes straight from the contract size.

What does “P&L per $1 move” mean?

It’s how much your position gains or loses for every $1 the price moves, based on the lot size and the pair’s contract size. For gold (1 lot = 100 oz), 0.01 lot = 1 oz, so it’s $1 per $1 move. For 0.001 lot it’s $0.10. Green is the gain if price rises $1, red the loss if it falls $1.

What is a lot, and why does 1 lot of gold equal 100 oz?

A lot is a standardised contract size. For metals, 1 standard lot is 100 troy oz (gold, platinum, palladium) or 5,000 oz (silver). For forex majors it’s 100,000 units of the base currency. For crypto futures it’s usually 1 coin (1 BTC, 1 ETH). The Contract Size Guide lists them all.

Is LotCal financial advice?

No. LotCal is an educational calculator. It does not recommend any trade, instrument or leverage level, and it is not a broker, advisor or platform. Read the full disclaimer.

Which pairs are supported?

Gold (XAUUSD), silver (XAGUSD), platinum, palladium, Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, XRP, BNB, Dogecoin, Cardano, Litecoin, and the main forex pairs — EURUSD, GBPUSD, AUDUSD, NZDUSD, USDJPY, USDCHF, USDCAD.

Why is the price field disabled for USDJPY, USDCHF and USDCAD?

For those pairs USD is the base currency, so position value in USD is simply lot × contract size — the price doesn’t enter the calculation. So the field is disabled and the hint explains it.

How accurate is the leverage number?

It’s exact arithmetic for the values you enter, but it is a theoretical estimate: it assumes your entire balance is initial margin and ignores fees, spread, and broker margin tiers. It will not match a real broker’s actual margin requirement exactly. Use it to understand the size of a position, not as a guaranteed quote.

Can I use it for spot trading?

Yes, with a caveat. Spot trades don’t use leverage the same way, but you can still enter your balance and size to see position value and quantity. The leverage figure is meaningful for margin and futures accounts.

Is there a mobile app?

No — LotCal is a responsive website that works on any phone browser. It’s deliberately small and fast.
