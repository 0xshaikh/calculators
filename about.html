What is LotCal?

A free, honest leverage calculator for people who trade forex, metals and crypto futures — built because I kept getting the arithmetic wrong.

LotCal kya hai

LotCal is a small, free, open calculator that answers one question I asked myself far too often: “if I open this lot size on this pair with this account, what leverage am I actually using — and how much do I win or lose per $1 of price movement?” You enter your balance, pick a pair, type a lot size and a price, and it tells you the position value, the quantity in oz / BTC / EUR, the required leverage, and the live P&L per $1 move in green and red.

Why I built it

I trade futures and I kept guessing. “Is 0.01 lot of gold a dollar a point? Two?” I’d do the mental math, get it wrong more often than I’d like, and find out the real number after the trade was already open. LotCal is the check I wanted before clicking buy or sell. I made it public because the same confusion is everywhere — and because a tool that removes guesswork before a trade is worth sharing.

Who’s behind it

I’m Anas Shaikh. I’m self-taught, curious about markets and automation, and I build small tools that answer real questions. LotCal is one of them. It’s not a company and it’s not a signal service — it’s one person’s calculator, published for anyone who wants to size a position before risking money.

How it works

LotCal uses the standard futures formula: position value = lot size × contract size × price, and leverage = position value ÷ balance. Contract sizes are common broker defaults — 1 lot of gold = 100 oz, 1 lot of EURUSD = 100,000 units, 1 lot of BTC = 1 BTC. Prices are reference snapshots, so replace them with your broker’s live quote. It is deliberately conservative: it assumes your whole balance is available as initial margin, so the leverage you see is the maximum, not a promise.

What it is not

LotCal is not financial advice, not a recommendation, and not a broker. It does not execute trades, hold funds, or know your broker’s margin tiers, fees or liquidation rules. It just does the arithmetic honestly and leaves the judgement to you.
