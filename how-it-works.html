Methodology

How It Works

The exact formula, the assumptions behind it, and what LotCal deliberately does not do.

What is leverage, and how does the calculator work?

Leverage lets you control a position worth far more than your account balance. With 1:100 leverage, $100 controls a $10,000 position — the broker lends you the difference, and your margin is the slice of your balance set aside as collateral. LotCal is deliberately conservative: it assumes your entire balance is available as initial margin, so the leverage you see is the maximum your account could possibly support.

The formula

quantity = lot size × contract size
position value = quantity × price (for USD-quote pairs)
position value = quantity (for USD-base pairs — price not used)
required leverage = position value ÷ account balance
P&L per $1 move = lot size × contract size (USD-quote pairs)

For USD-base pairs (USDJPY, USDCHF, USDCAD) the position value in USD is lot × contract size, so the price field is disabled and no P&L per $1 is shown (a $1 move in JPY isn’t a $1 move in USD).

Example 1 — Gold (XAUUSD)

1 lot of gold is 100 troy oz. On a $30 balance with 0.01 lot at $4,100:

  • Quantity = 0.01 × 100 = 1 oz
  • Position value = 1 × $4,100 = $4,100
  • Required leverage = $4,100 ÷ $30 ≈ 137X

Your broker must offer at least ~137:1 leverage to open that trade on a $30 account.

Example 2 — Euro (EURUSD)

1 lot of EURUSD is 100,000 EUR. On a $1,000 balance with 0.1 lot at 1.1232:

  • Quantity = 0.1 × 100,000 = 10,000 EUR
  • Position value = 10,000 × 1.1232 = $11,232
  • Required leverage = $11,232 ÷ $1,000 ≈ 11.2X

P&L per $1 move

Because 1 gold lot = 100 oz, a 0.01 lot is exactly 1 oz — so every $1 gold moves, your P&L changes by $1. The result shows this as +$1.00 / −$1.00 (green / red). For a 0.001 lot it is $0.10 per $1 move, and for 0.005 lot it is $0.50. That live figure is the per-lot sensitivity of the position, straight from the contract size.

Why it matters

Higher leverage means bigger swings on the same balance. At 137X, a 1% adverse price move wipes out roughly the whole margin you put up — and on some brokers losses can exceed your deposit. LotCal shows the arithmetic so you can size positions before you click buy or sell. It is education, not a recommendation.

Assumptions

  • Contract sizes are common broker defaults: 1 lot gold/platinum/palladium = 100 oz, silver = 5,000 oz, forex = 100,000 units, crypto = 1 coin.
  • Margin: your entire balance is assumed available as initial margin. Real brokers reserve more (margin calls, maintenance margin), so the real requirement is higher.
  • Prices are reference snapshots (4–6 Nov 2026) and are not live — replace them with your broker’s quote.
  • No fees — spread, commission, swap and funding are ignored.

Limits

  • It does not know your broker’s margin tiers, leverage cap, spread or liquidation rules.
  • It does not predict price, place trades or hold funds.
  • Results are estimates for education, not a guarantee a broker will accept the order.
  • It is not financial advice.
