Silver Contract Size: XAGUSD Lots, Ounces, and Dollars

LotCal Blog6 min read

Silver is a much bigger contract than gold

If you have traded gold and are now looking at silver, the first thing to know is that the contract sizes are not even in the same ballpark. A standard lot of gold controls 100 troy ounces. A standard lot of silver controls 5,000 troy ounces.

That is fifty times more metal per lot. It is the single biggest reason the two markets feel so different to trade, even though they are both precious metals and often move in the same direction on the same news.

The numbers behind 5,000 ounces

Silver quotes in troy ounces, the same unit as gold — roughly 31.1 grams each. When you buy 1 lot of XAGUSD, you are controlling 5,000 of them. Let's turn that into the two numbers that actually matter.

Position value

Position value is ounces multiplied by the price. Silver trades in the low $30s at the time of writing, so:

  • 1.00 lot = 5,000 oz. At $32: 5000 × 32 = $160,000.
  • 0.10 lot = 500 oz. At $32: 500 × 32 = $16,000.
  • 0.01 lot = 50 oz. At $32: 50 × 32 = $1,600.

Notice the contrast with gold. On gold, 0.01 lot was 1 ounce worth about $4,100. On silver, 0.01 lot is 50 ounces worth about $1,600. Silver gives you far more metal for the same lot number, but at a much lower price per ounce — so the position value can be smaller even though the contract is far larger in ounces.

Profit and loss per $1 move

Because one lot controls 5,000 ounces, every $1 move in the silver price moves your P&L by $5,000 per lot. The formula:

P&L per $1 move = lot size × 5,000

  • 1.00 lot → $5,000 per $1 move.
  • 0.10 lot → $500 per $1.
  • 0.01 lot → $50 per $1.
  • 0.001 lot → $5 per $1.

That is the headline difference. On gold, 0.01 lot gives you $1 of P&L per $1 of price movement. On silver, the same 0.01 lot gives you $50 per $1 move. The silver contract is not just bigger in ounces — it makes your P&L far more sensitive to the exact same price change.

Why silver feels so much more volatile per lot

This is the part that catches people out. Silver is not necessarily more volatile in percentage terms than gold — it is often comparable — but the 5,000-ounce contract means every percentage move translates into a large dollar swing, and silver's daily range has historically been wider than gold's. Both effects point the same way.

Let's put numbers on it. A 1% move in gold at $4,100 is $41. On a 1.00 lot, that is 100 oz × 41 = $4,100. A 1% move in silver at $32 is $0.32. On a 1.00 lot, that is 5000 × 0.32 = $1,600. So the same 1% move swings $4,100 on gold and $1,600 on silver per full lot.

But here is the subtlety that matters for sizing: silver's percentage range is often wider. If silver moves 3% in a day — a normal day for it — that is about $0.96, which on 0.01 lot is 50 × 0.96 ≈ $48. On a small account, that single day is a big chunk. The contract size is what turns an ordinary daily swing into a meaningful P&L hit.

A worked example

Say you have a $1,000 account and you buy 0.01 lot of silver at $32. Position value: 50 × 32 = $1,600. So you are controlling $1,600 with $1,000 — under 2× leverage, fairly conservative as leveraged metals go. But look at the P&L sensitivity: each $1 of price movement is $50. Silver can easily move a dollar in a day, and a single $1 adverse move is $50, or 5% of your account.

Push it further. If silver moves $2 against you, that is $100 — 10% of your account. And if you had sized up to 0.10 lot, the same $2 move would be $1,000, which would take the entire account. The contract size is the reason these numbers scale so fast, and it is why the lot number you choose matters far more on silver than on a market with a smaller contract.

Fractional lots and the "50 ounce" shortcut

Just like gold, silver gives you an easy shortcut: 0.01 lot = 50 ounces. Every 0.01 lot you add is another 50 ounces. So 0.05 lot is 250 oz, 0.10 lot is 500 oz, and so on.

Some brokers let you go to 0.001 lot (5 ounces) or even lower. The math still holds: 0.001 lot's P&L per $1 move is 0.001 × 5000 = $5.

For any lot number, the pattern is the same: multiply by 5,000 to get the ounces, multiply the ounces by the price for position value, and multiply the lot number by 5,000 again for the per-dollar P&L.

Check your broker's contract

As with any market, 5,000 ounces is the standard but not the only option. Some brokers and some exchanges define a silver lot differently. The CME's silver futures contract is also 5,000 troy ounces, but spot silver CFD contracts can vary from platform to platform. Always confirm the number before you size anything.

Bottom line

Silver's contract size is the single biggest reason it feels different from gold. One lot is 5,000 ounces; P&L moves $5,000 per $1 on a full lot; and a tiny 0.01 lot still swings $50 per $1 of price movement. Know the contract size, respect the per-move sensitivity, and you can size silver positions with both eyes open instead of finding out the hard way.
